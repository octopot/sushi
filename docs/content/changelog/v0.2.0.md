# v0.2.0 — A new name, a key of its own.

Susi is now **Sushi**, named like the rest of its family. The panel has a key of its own: **Caps Lock** shows and hides it, because macOS 27 gives ⌘⇧Space to Visual Intelligence and 1Password used the same keys. The first launch of Sushi brings over your actions, your dictionary, your library and the prompt you were writing, so you continue where you stopped.

Pick your path: [you have Susi](#upgrade-from-susi), or [you are new to Sushi](#new-to-sushi).

## Upgrade from Susi

Five steps in Terminal, about two minutes. Your settings, your library and the prompt in the panel stay on your Mac the whole time.

**1. Update Homebrew's list of apps.**

```sh
brew update
```

**2. Upgrade by the new name, in full.**

```sh
brew upgrade --cask octolab/tap/sushi
```

Homebrew quits Susi, removes `Susi.app` and puts `Sushi.app` in Applications. You should see `Upgrading susi` and `Moving App 'Sushi.app'`. Type the name in full, `octolab/tap/sushi`: Homebrew trusts apps from other taps one name at a time, and the full name in this command is what lets it load the new one. A plain `brew upgrade` may refuse it as untrusted.

**3. Start Sushi.**

```sh
open -a Sushi
```

Homebrew does not reopen it this time: it looks for Susi, which is gone, and may print `LSCopyApplicationURLsForBundleIdentifier() failed` for `me.octopot.susi`. That line is harmless. Sushi's icon appears in the menu bar. If macOS says it cannot verify Sushi, open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway** next to the message about Sushi; macOS asks once.

**4. Press Caps Lock.**

The panel shows the prompt you left in Susi, with its versions. Check that the rest came along:

- **⌘L** lists your prompts and templates;
- **⌘K** lists your actions;
- **Open Settings Folder** in the menu bar menu opens `~/Library/Application Support/me.octopot.sushi`, with your `rules.json` and `dictionary.json`.

If the line above the bottom bar says something could not be brought over, nothing is lost: Susi's files are where they were, and Sushi tries again at the next launch.

**5. Tidy up, once everything is there.** Sushi copies Susi's folders and never deletes them. When you are sure nothing is missing, remove them yourself:

```sh
rm -rf ~/Library/Application\ Support/me.octopot.susi ~/Library/WebKit/me.octopot.susi
```

Homebrew also keeps a record of the old name, so `brew list --cask` shows `susi` next to `sushi`. It is not an app, only a note in Homebrew's folder; remove it once `brew list --cask` shows `sushi`:

```sh
rm -rf "$(brew --caskroom)/susi"
```

### Good to know

- **Do not run `brew uninstall --cask susi`.** The old name now leads to Sushi, so that command removes Sushi. To clear `susi` from `brew list`, use the last command of step 5.
- **Login Items.** If you added Susi to **System Settings → General → Login Items**, remove it there and add Sushi.
- **Installed from the zip?** Quit Susi, download `sushi_0.2.0_darwin-arm64.zip` from the [release](https://github.com/octopot/sushi/releases/tag/v0.2.0), move `Sushi.app` to Applications and open it; it brings your data over the same way. Then move `Susi.app` to the Trash.
- **Want ⌘⇧Space back?** Turn **Use Caps Lock** off in the menu bar menu and put `"shortcut": "Command+Shift+Space"` in `hotkey.json` in the settings folder. On macOS 27 these keys open Visual Intelligence instead. [How the key works →](/guide/keyboard/#change-the-key)

## New to Sushi

**1. Install Sushi.**

```sh
brew install --cask octolab/tap/sushi
```

**2. Download the local model** that edits your text. It runs in [Ollama](https://ollama.com/download) on your Mac.

```sh
ollama pull qwen3.5:4b-mlx
```

**3. Start Sushi.**

```sh
open -a Sushi
```

The panel appears, and Sushi's icon appears in the menu bar.

**4. Write your first prompt.** Press **Caps Lock** over any app, type or dictate, select a phrase and press **⌘K** to rewrite it, then **⌘↵** to copy the prompt and return to your agent.

## Caps Lock shows Sushi

- One press of **Caps Lock** shows the panel over any app; another press hides it. Holding the key does not repeat.
- While Sushi runs, Caps Lock does not switch to capitals. Quitting Sushi gives it back.
- No permission to grant: Sushi maps Caps Lock to a key no keyboard has, on every keyboard, including one you connect later, and leaves your other key mappings alone.
- The menu bar menu shows the key in use and has **Use Caps Lock**. Turn it off to keep Caps Lock for capitals; then **⌃⇧Space** shows Sushi.
- If macOS switches input sources with Caps Lock, or another app already remaps it, such as Raycast's Hyper Key, Sushi uses **⌃⇧Space** and says why.
- Both choices live in `hotkey.json` in the settings folder: any combination of modifiers and one key.

## A new name

- The app is `Sushi.app`, its settings folder is `~/Library/Application Support/me.octopot.sushi`, and the Homebrew cask is `octolab/tap/sushi`.
- The site moved to [sushi.octolab.org](/) and the code to [github.com/octopot/sushi](https://github.com/octopot/sushi).
- The panel, the keys inside it, the library and the local model work as before.

**Upgrade:** from Susi, follow [the five steps above](#upgrade-from-susi). From now on, `brew upgrade --cask octolab/tap/sushi` quits Sushi, replaces it and opens it again.
